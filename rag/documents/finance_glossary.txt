Price to Earnings Ratio (P/E Ratio)

The Price to Earnings ratio, commonly called the P/E ratio, is a valuation metric used to compare a company's current share price with its earnings per share.

Formula:

P/E Ratio = Market Price per Share / Earnings per Share (EPS)

Interpretation:

A high P/E ratio may indicate that investors expect higher future growth, or that the stock is expensive relative to its current earnings.

A low P/E ratio may indicate that the stock is cheaper relative to its earnings, though it may also reflect lower growth expectations or business risk.

The P/E ratio should not be used alone. Investors normally compare it with:

- historical P/E of the company
- industry P/E
- peer companies
- earnings growth
- business quality
- future growth expectations

Example:

If a company's share price is ₹500 and its earnings per share is ₹25:

P/E Ratio = 500 / 25 = 20

This means investors are paying ₹20 for every ₹1 of annual earnings generated by the company.
